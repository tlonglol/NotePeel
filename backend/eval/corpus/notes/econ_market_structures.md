## Perfect Competition
Many small firms, identical products, no barriers to entry, perfect information. Firms are price takers (demand curve is horizontal at market price). Profit max where MR = MC and P = MR. Long run: economic profit = 0 b/c entry drives price down to min ATC. Allocatively efficient (P = MC) and productively efficient. ex agriculture (close to it).

## Monopoly
One seller, unique product, high barriers to entry (patents, natural monopoly, control of a resource). Price maker, faces the whole downward sloping market demand, MR < P. Still produces where MR = MC but charges the price off the demand curve above that. Result: higher price, lower quantity than competition, deadweight loss, not allocatively efficient. Price discrimination if they can separate customers.

## Monopolistic Competition
Many firms, DIFFERENTIATED products (restaurants, clothing brands), low barriers. Some price setting power from branding. Zero economic profit in the long run like perfect competition but w/ excess capacity and P > MC.

## Oligopoly
A few large firms, interdependent (each watches the others), high barriers. Game theory / prisoners dilemma explains why cartels like OPEC cheat. Kinked demand curve -> sticky prices. Collusion is illegal in the US (antitrust laws, Sherman Act).
