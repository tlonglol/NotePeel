## Ionic Bonds
Transfer of electrons from a metal to a nonmetal. Forms ions: cation (+) and anion (-), held together by electrostatic attraction. Ex: NaCl, Na gives 1 e- to Cl. Properties: high melting point, crystal lattice, conduct electricity when dissolved or molten, brittle.

## Covalent Bonds
Sharing of electrons between two nonmetals. Single bond = 1 pair shared, double = 2 pairs, triple = 3 pairs (triple is shortest and strongest). Ex: $H_2O$, $CO_2$, $CH_4$. Properties: lower melting pts, dont conduct, can be gases/liquids.

## Polar vs Nonpolar
- electronegativity difference decides it
- diff > 1.7 -> ionic
- 0.4 to 1.7 -> polar covalent (unequal sharing, partial charges $\delta^+$ $\delta^-$)
- < 0.4 -> nonpolar covalent (equal sharing)
- water is polar b/c O is much more electroneg than H, bent shape

## Metallic Bonds
sea of delocalized electrons around metal cations -> conductive, malleable, ductile
