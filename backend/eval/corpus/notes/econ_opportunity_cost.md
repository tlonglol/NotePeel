## Scarcity and Choice
Economics = study of how people make choices under scarcity (unlimited wants, limited resources). Every choice has an opportunity cost = the value of the NEXT BEST alternative given up, not all the alternatives. Ex: going to college costs tuition PLUS the wages you could have earned.

## Production Possibilities Frontier
Curve showing max combos of 2 goods an economy can produce w/ all resources fully employed. Points on the curve = efficient, inside = unemployment/inefficiency, outside = unattainable right now. Bowed out shape b/c of increasing opportunity cost (resources arent equally good at everything). Straight line PPF = constant opportunity cost. Growth (more resources or better tech) shifts the whole curve outward.

## Absolute vs Comparative Advantage
- absolute advantage: can produce more w/ the same resources
- comparative advantage: can produce at a LOWER OPPORTUNITY COST. this is what trade is based on
- countries should specialize in their comparative advantage and trade, both gain even if one has absolute advantage in everything
- calculate opp cost as "give up / gain", output problems: other over, input problems: it over

## Marginal Thinking
rational people compare marginal benefit to marginal cost. do it if MB > MC. sunk costs are irrelevant to the decision (already gone)
