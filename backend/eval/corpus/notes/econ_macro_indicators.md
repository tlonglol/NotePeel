## GDP
Total market value of all FINAL goods and services produced within a country in a year. Expenditure approach: $GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)$. Consumption is about 70% of US GDP. Excludes: used goods, intermediate goods (double counting), financial transactions, non market work, underground economy. Nominal = current prices, real = adjusted for inflation (use real to compare years). GDP per capita for standard of living. Recession = 2 consecutive quarters of falling real GDP.

## Inflation
General rise in the price level. Measured by CPI (basket of goods a typical urban consumer buys). inflation rate = % change in CPI. Demand pull (too much money chasing too few goods) vs cost push (input costs rise, oil shock). Hurts savers and lenders and people on fixed incomes, helps borrowers. Fed targets about 2%. Deflation is also bad (people wait to buy). Hyperinflation ex Germany 1923, Zimbabwe.

## Unemployment
unemployment rate = unemployed / labor force x 100. Labor force = employed + unemployed (actively looking). Discouraged workers who stopped looking are NOT counted, so the rate understates it.
- frictional: between jobs, normal, short term
- structural: skills dont match jobs, technology, long term
- cyclical: from a recession, the bad kind, zero at full employment
- natural rate = frictional + structural, about 4-5% in the US
- Okuns law relates unemployment to the GDP gap
