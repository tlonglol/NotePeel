## Reaction Rates
Rate = change in concentration / time. Collision theory: particles must collide w/ enough energy (activation energy $E_a$) and correct orientation.

## Factors that Increase Rate
- higher concentration -> more collisions
- higher temperature -> more energetic collisions
- more surface area (powder vs chunk)
- catalyst -> lowers activation energy, not consumed, enzymes are biological catalysts
- rate law: rate = $k[A]^m[B]^n$, orders found experimentally not from coefficients

## Equilibrium
Forward rate = reverse rate, concentrations stop changing (but reactions still happening, dynamic). $K_{eq} = \frac{[products]^{coeff}}{[reactants]^{coeff}}$. K > 1 favors products, K < 1 favors reactants. Solids and pure liquids left out.

## Le Chateliers Principle
- system shifts to relieve a stress
- add reactant -> shifts right, remove product -> shifts right
- increase pressure -> shifts to side w/ fewer moles of gas
- increase temp -> shifts away from heat (endothermic shifts right, exothermic shifts left)
- catalyst does NOT shift equilibrium, only gets there faster
