## Individual Gas Laws
- Boyle's law: $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2$ (constant T), pressure and volume inversely related
- Charles's law: $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ (constant P), volume and temp directly related, T in KELVIN
- Gay-Lussac: $\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$ (constant V)
- Combined: $\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$
- Avogadro: V proportional to n

## Ideal Gas Law
$PV = nRT$ where R = 0.0821 L atm / mol K (or 8.314 J / mol K). K = C + 273. 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 101.3 kPa. Ideal gas assumes no volume for particles and no intermolecular attractions, works best at high T and low P.

## Daltons Law of Partial Pressures
$P_{total} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 ...$ each gas acts independently. Mole fraction $X_A = \frac{n_A}{n_{total}}$, $P_A = X_A P_{total}$

## Kinetic Molecular Theory
gas particles in constant random motion, collisions elastic, avg KE proportional to Kelvin temp. Graham's law: lighter gases effuse faster, rate proportional to $1/\sqrt{M}$
