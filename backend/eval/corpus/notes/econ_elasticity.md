## Price Elasticity of Demand
How responsive quantity demanded is to a price change. $$E_d = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta P}$$ (use absolute value). Midpoint formula for percent changes so it doesnt matter which direction you go.

## Interpreting
- $|E_d| > 1$ elastic - quantity responds a lot (luxuries, many substitutes, big share of budget, long time horizon)
- $|E_d| < 1$ inelastic - quantity barely responds (necessities, gas, insulin, few substitutes, short run)
- $|E_d| = 1$ unit elastic
- perfectly inelastic = vertical demand curve, perfectly elastic = horizontal

## Elasticity and Total Revenue
TR = P x Q. If demand is elastic, raising price LOWERS revenue (lose too many customers). If inelastic, raising price RAISES revenue. Thats why cigarette and gas taxes raise money. Along a straight line demand curve elasticity changes: elastic at the top, inelastic at the bottom, revenue maxed at unit elastic.

## Other Elasticities
- income elasticity: positive for normal goods, negative for inferior
- cross price elasticity: positive for substitutes, negative for complements
- elasticity of supply: how responsive Qs is, higher in the long run
- tax incidence: the more inelastic side bears more of the tax burden
